SICP doesn't teach you a language — it teaches you computation itself. Every chapter strips away another layer of abstraction until you're left with the raw mechanics of how programs work.

## What stuck with me

The emphasis on **abstraction barriers** — building systems in layers where each layer only knows what it needs to — is something I apply to every architecture I design.

The chapter on metalinguistic abstraction, where you build an interpreter for Scheme *in* Scheme, is one of those moments where programming stops being a skill and starts being a way of thinking.

## Why it matters

Most programming books teach you syntax. This one teaches you structure. If you want to build systems that scale — not just in performance, but in clarity — this is where you start.
