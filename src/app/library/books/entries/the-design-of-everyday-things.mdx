Don Norman rewired the way I look at every object I interact with. Doors, stoves, light switches — suddenly everything became a design problem worth solving.

## What stuck with me

The concept of **affordances** — that objects should signal how they're meant to be used — is something I now apply to every interface I build. If a user needs a manual, the design failed.

**Mapping** and **feedback** are the two principles I think about most. Good mapping means controls relate naturally to their effects. Good feedback means the system always tells you what's happening.

## Why it matters

Whether you're designing software, hardware, or physical products, Norman's framework is universal. The best designs are invisible — they just work. That's the standard I hold myself to.
