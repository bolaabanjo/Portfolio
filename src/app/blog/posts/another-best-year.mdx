So many Ws this year, can't even remember some.

I'm writing this on December 31st because I realized I hadn't stopped to count. The year moved fast. And somewhere between shipping code and studying for exams, 2025 became another entry in what I'm now calling the "best year" catalog.

Walk with me.

---

## Building: The Year I Started Companies

This was the year I stopped building for other people and started building for myself.

It began with FohnAI — an AI research and development company that became the umbrella for everything I'm doing in this space. The paperwork, the structure, the legitimacy of having something with a name and a direction. It's still early, but it's real.

Then came Cencori. Months of research, reading papers, false starts, and finally — the first real commits. A unified AI infrastructure platform. Security, cost tracking, multi-provider support. The kind of thing I kept wishing existed until I realized I'd have to build it myself.

The validation came faster than expected. YC-backed startups onboarded. Real production traffic. Real usage. The kind of proof you can't fake.

I also wrote my first check this year — $3,000. Not life-changing money, but everything in mine. The first time I saw real proof that something I built could turn into something I earned. That shift (from trading hours for money to creating value that compounds) changed how I think about everything else.

---

## Learning: The Year I Went Back to School

I've always believed you can teach yourself anything. This year, I tested that belief, and also admitted its limits.

I got accepted into MIT Professional Education to study AI. The email hit different. Name aside, because of what it represented. A door I wasn't sure would open.

I also got into Nigeria's biggest MBA program. Because the technical skills were never going to be enough. I needed to understand how companies actually run, not just how to build parts of them.

Four exams this year. Four passes. Not "barely." Passed.

On the self-taught side: systems design, DevOps, product architecture. The boring infrastructure work that makes the exciting stuff possible. I studied it because I needed it, and because the people who've done it at scale were generous enough to share what they knew.

---

## Creating: The Year I Shipped in Public

I launched my portfolio — bolabanjo.xyz. A place to collect everything I've been building. People actually liked it. Some said it inspired them to build their own. That wasn't the goal, but it's the best kind of side effect.

I started posting actively on X in February. Stopped lurking, started sharing. Even if I wasn't really active for the first 6 Months, The compound effects were real. The conversations that came from sharing openly changed my trajectory more than any tutorial ever did.

I also created an energy system this year — an Energy System that could potentially rival existing solutions. It got attention from researchers, professors, and publishers. One of my proudest moments. The early work is on [GitHub](https://github.com/bolaabanjo/quantonic-reactor).

And I made music again. Not for streams this time but just because I missed it. Creation takes many forms, and sometimes you need to express something that code can't capture.

---

## Growing: The Year I Changed Internally

Some of the biggest wins this year weren't visible.

I got inducted into the Engineering Society of Nigeria. A ceremony, a title, a formal recognition of something I'd been doing in the dark for years. There's something strange about being "officially" what you already know you are. But it mattered.

I assembled a team — not a company team, but a core group of people I trust to build with. The kind of people who push you without competing with you. Who show up when it's not convenient.

I worked at two startups as a design engineer. Different products, different cultures, same lesson: design is engineering, engineering is design. The lines blur when you're actually building.

And I found God again. Quietly, Deeply. In the spaces between the wins and the work. It just happened, and it mattered. None of these would have happened without God.

---

## The Pattern

Every year (2019, 2021, 2023, 2024 and now 2025), I think I've peaked. And every year, the next one makes the last one look like a warm-up.

I don't know if that's luck, momentum, or just refusing to stop when I'm tired. Probably all three.

What I do know is this: I'm not the same person who walked into January. The growth isn't always visible. But when you zoom out (when you actually list it) it's impossible to deny.

---

## What's Next

I don't do resolutions. And I'm not even trying to sound robotic like everybody else, I genuinly just do not give a fuck. 
I only set my goals and go after it ruthlessly.
2026 is about depth over breadth. Fewer projects, more impact. Less starting, more finishing. Building the things that compound.
Cencori becomes a real product. FohnAI scales. The catalog keeps growing.
Another year added to the "best year" catalog.

Life is good.
