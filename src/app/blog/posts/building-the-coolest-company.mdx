I want to build the coolest company ever built.

Not “cool” as in merch drops and a nice office, though we’ll have those too. Cool as in: the engineering is sharp enough to make other engineers jealous. The design is intentional down to the corners nobody looks at. The culture burns. And the people inside it walk into other rooms (conferences, interviews, dinners) and quietly intimidate everyone there. 

And this won’t even be because they’re loud, but it’s going to be obvious, within about ten minutes of talking to them, that they think harder and care more than the people across the table. That kind of cool.

As CEO, I’ve come to accept that Cencori is a mirror of me. Every company is a mirror of its founder eventually, and pretending otherwise is how you end up with a culture deck that nobody believes. My intensity is the engine of this thing. It’s how I’ve won, over and over, in rooms where I had no business winning. It’s what’s gotten Cencori from an idea to something real. And it’s what the team will have to match, in their own way, for us to win at the scale I’m actually aiming for.

But mirrors cut both ways.
The same intensity that ships product has, more than once, cost me my own health. I know what it feels like to run myself into the ground in service of something I believe in, and I know I have a blind spot about how that looks from the outside — about how the standards I hold myself to land on the people I lead. The people who trust me with their minds, their hours, and their best years deserve a leader who notices when his own pace starts getting quietly passed down to them. If it ever does, that won’t be their problem but mine. That’s me failing at the job. Holding the line on intensity and on the wellbeing of the people producing it is the actual work of running a company. Anyone can do one. Doing both is the bar.

We’re not a family (lol, Please.) We’re a team — different brains, different skill gaps, different ways of executing, and that difference is exactly what makes the thing whole. I don’t need everyone to think like me. I need everyone to care about the work the way I care about the work, and to bring something to it that I couldn’t bring myself. The team I’m building isn’t a group of people who agree with me. It’s a group of people who make me look slower than I am.
We’re not there yet. Not even close. Right now we’re a small group of people building something most of the market doesn’t fully understand yet, and a lot of days the gap between where we are and where I want us to be is the loudest thing in my head.

But that’s the build. The coolest company ever, with the right people in the room, doing work that’s obviously sharper than what anyone else is shipping — led by a person honest enough to know which parts of himself to scale across the company, and which parts to keep on a leash.

That’s the job. I’m in.
